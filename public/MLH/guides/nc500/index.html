01 / THE ESCAPE

Give the journey room

The NC500 is an invitation to slow down. For a trip from Manchester, our suggested starting point is ten to fourteen days, including the journeys to and from the Highlands. Give yourself an overnight stop on the way north, then arrive around Inverness ready to begin rather than tired from a long drive. Sketch out a few overnight bases before filling in the days between them. Two nights somewhere you love can be more memorable than another hundred miles. Treat this as a flexible holiday outline, with room for weather, rest and the places you discover along the way.

02 / THE ESCAPE

Build a gentler loop

Build your loop around a handful of areas: the east coast, the far north around Durness, and the west through Assynt and Wester Ross. You do not need to tick off every viewpoint. Pick one main outing each day, then leave the afternoon open for a beach walk, a village stop or an early return to camp. The official NC500 guidance suggests at least five to seven days on the route itself; extra time makes the journey from Manchester more comfortable. Before setting off each morning, check your next campsite’s arrival window and the route that actually suits your vehicle.

03 / THE ESCAPE

Make the road part of the holiday

Coastal roads can demand more attention than the map suggests. Read the official campervan advice before choosing mountain passes or narrow detours, and confirm any vehicle restrictions with us before travel. Passing places need to stay clear for traffic, so save photo stops for suitable parking areas. Stock up on food before moving between bases and keep your fuel stops in mind. A simple supper back at camp is part of the pleasure: boots by the door, a warm drink and a different view from the same little kitchen. Keep a lower-level, shorter alternative ready for days when the weather changes.

04 / THE ESCAPE

A good night, a relaxed return

Book recognised campsites or authorised overnight stops for every night and check that they accept your camper’s dimensions. Scotland’s outdoor access rights do not give motor vehicles a general right to overnight wherever they choose. Use the facilities provided for fresh water and waste, and leave each stop ready for the next visitor. For the journey home, resist planning a final remote night followed by a rushed return. An easier last stage leaves time for packing, refuelling and the agreed handover. The best souvenir is a trip you enjoyed all the way through, including the drive back.

Before you go

Check current access, conditions and opening information before travelling. This itinerary is a starting point, with campsites and activities booked separately.
