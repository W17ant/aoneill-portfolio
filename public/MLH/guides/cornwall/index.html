THE MLH TRAVEL JOURNAL

A week of coastal walks, harbour mornings and unhurried evenings back at camp. A starting point for your own adventure, with time to settle in and space to change your plans.

01 / THE ESCAPE

Choose your coastal base

Cornwall rewards a little commitment to doing less. For a week away from Manchester, choose one or two campsite bases instead of moving every night. Allow a generous travel day at either end, with breaks and a realistic arrival time. Your first evening can be as simple as unpacking, putting the kettle on and finding your bearings. Book a pitch that suits your camper, check whether you need an electric hook-up and ask about the approach road. If the campsite recommends a particular route, follow it rather than a shortcut through lanes you have not driven before.

02 / THE ESCAPE

Follow the Atlantic coast

Make the north coast your base for open Atlantic views and days built around the sea. Around the Newquay area, choose a coastal walk or a beach visit as the day’s main event, leaving room for lunch and a relaxed journey back. The coastline near Bedruthan and Tintagel makes a fine subject for a camera, but check current path and attraction access before setting out. A photograph does not tell you whether a route is open. Pack a windproof layer even when camp feels warm, and choose the length of your walk for the whole group rather than the most enthusiastic walker.

03 / THE ESCAPE

Find a different rhythm

For a change of pace, spend part of the week around the south coast. Falmouth and Fowey offer a different setting for a harbour stroll and a long lunch. Pick one area to explore rather than trying to cross the county several times in a day. Leave the camper in suitable parking and wander on foot where practical. Build in an afternoon with no destination at all: a book, something good to cook and time outside your own door. On wet days, a nearby indoor visit or a café can be enough. There is no need to rescue every day with another long drive.

04 / THE ESCAPE

Leave room for the journey home

Before each beach outing, check local tide times, conditions and the guidance at that beach. Confirm seasonal dog restrictions if you are travelling with a pet, and use marked access routes. Overnight stays need their own plan: reserve a campsite and follow its arrival and waste-disposal instructions. Keep your last night within a comfortable journey of your return, rather than trying to squeeze in one more distant stop. Pack away sandy equipment, leave time to refuel and make the final morning an easy one. A week can feel wonderfully full without a full itinerary.

Before you go

Check current access, conditions and opening information before travelling. This itinerary is a starting point, with campsites and activities booked separately.
